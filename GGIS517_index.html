Project Overview

This interactive map introduces Chicago’s Tunnel and Reservoir Plan (TARP), a major underground infrastructure system designed to reduce flooding and improve water quality. Use the zoom buttons in the side panel to explore individual tunnel systems and related facilities, then open each section to learn how the system works and why it matters.

  • Location: Near O’Hare Airport
  • Scale: About 6.6 miles of tunnels
  • Communities served: Arlington Heights, Des Plaines, and Mount Prospect
  • Connected facility: Gloria Alitto Majewski Reservoir
  • Why it matters: Helps reduce local flooding and capture sewer overflow
  • Location: Western and northwestern Chicago region
  • Scale: About 26.6 miles of tunnels
  • Connected facility: McCook Reservoir
  • Main role: Stores excess stormwater and sewage during heavy rainfall
  • Why it matters: Helps reduce street flooding and polluted overflow
  • Scale: Largest tunnel system in TARP, about 40.5 miles long
  • Service area: Covers a large part of the Chicago combined sewer area
  • Connected facility: McCook Reservoir
  • Main role: Captures large volumes of stormwater during major rain events
  • Why it matters: One of the most important parts of the overall TARP network
  • Location: Southern part of the TARP service area
  • Scale: About 36.7 miles of tunnels
  • Connected facility: Thornton Reservoir
  • Main role: Helps manage stormwater and sewage during storms
  • Why it matters: Supports both flood control and water quality protection
  • Full name: Tunnel and Reservoir Plan (TARP)
  • Also called: Chicago Deep Tunnel
  • Main purpose: Reduce flooding and improve water quality
  • Problem addressed: In older parts of Chicago, stormwater and sewage share the same pipes
  • Why it was needed: Heavy rain can overload the sewer system and cause overflow into streets, basements, and waterways

Learn more on official website

  1. Heavy rain causes the sewer system to fill up.
  2. Excess flow is diverted into deep tunnels.
  3. Water is stored underground in tunnels and reservoirs.
  4. Water reclamation plants treat the stored water when capacity is available.
  5. Flooding and polluted overflow are reduced.
  • Total tunnel length: About 110 miles
  • Total reservoir storage: About 15.15 billion gallons
  • Service area: Roughly 352 square miles
  • People served: About 3.75 million
  • Project value: One of the largest civil engineering projects in the Chicago region
  • McCook Reservoir Stage 2 is still under construction and is the major unfinished part of the system.
  • TARP is not only a flood-control project; it also helps reduce combined sewer overflow pollution.
  • The map focuses on major systems and facilities, so it is an introduction rather than a full engineering inventory.
